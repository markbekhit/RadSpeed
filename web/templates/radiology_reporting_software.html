A practical buying checklist

What to look for in radiology reporting software

A reporting tool earns its place when it shortens the loop around the radiologist. These are the parts worth testing in a real reporting session.

01

Speech that tolerates radiology

Test subspecialty vocabulary, short corrections and the terms your own list produces. RadSpeed can use streaming medical transcription providers and learns repeated vocabulary corrections per user.

02

Structure without a house style

Your headings, spelling, measurements, laterality and impression format should remain recognisable. RadSpeed includes 40 templates and supports user-defined reporting conventions.

03

A safe path between cases

Patient context must change atomically. Prior text should enter the prompt only when selected. Finished text must move back to the reporting system without a complicated detour.

04

Checks that stay advisory

Laterality, anatomy, modality, gender and unit checks can identify review points. They should flag the issue, not silently rewrite the report.

05

A visible report history

Drafts, sign-off and amendments need a traceable sequence. RadSpeed preserves report versions and writes a tamper-evident event history.

06

Integration claims you can verify

Ask what works today and what still needs local configuration. RadSpeed supports smart paste now and includes HL7, DICOM SR, worklist and FHIR foundations for configured practice integrations.

Honest limits

Assistance is useful only when its limits stay visible.

It does not make the clinical decision.

Generated text and QA flags require radiologist review. RadSpeed does not claim diagnostic accuracy or autonomous reporting.

Integration is not one universal switch.

Practice connections depend on the local RIS, PACS, security model and integration pathway. Available standards support that work; they do not replace it.

Benchmarks need a method.

Any published RadSpeed benchmark must use synthetic or public data, state its method and avoid implying clinical validation.
