### THIS IS THE SAMPLE TEMPLATE TO BE USED FOR THE TRANSCRIPT ###

### Exam:
MRI Lumbar Spine

### Technique:
MRI of the lumbar spine was performed using T1-weighted, T2-weighted, and STIR sequences in sagittal and axial planes. [If contrast: Post-gadolinium T1-weighted sequences were also obtained.]

### Clinical Details
**Instructions:**
- Record clinical details exactly as dictated. Do not rephrase or reformulate the wording.
- **Exclude** patient name, date of birth, age, gender, and MRN/accession numbers from the report, even if mentioned in the transcript.
- **If not provided:** 'No clinical details provided.'

### Comparison:
**Instructions:**
- **If provided:** List the prior imaging studies mentioned (modality and date/year if stated). Do not summarise changes here — comparison findings belong in the Findings section.
- **If not provided:** 'No prior imaging available for comparison.'

### Findings:
**Instructions:**
- Use this exact internal order within Findings: **overview first, then levels, then final checks**. Do not create extra structure-by-structure subsections.
- **Overview:** Begin with one concise unlabelled paragraph covering lumbar alignment (including lordosis, scoliosis or listhesis) and vertebral body heights/bone marrow signal. Follow with one concise sentence stating the conus termination level and signal. Preserve dictated wording when supplied; otherwise use compact normal wording.
- **Levels:** Then report L1/2, L2/3, L3/4, L4/5 and L5/S1 in anatomical order, regardless of dictation order. Give every level its own bold heading, e.g. **L4/5:**. Gather every comment for that level together. Include disc morphology and signal, endplate change, facet arthropathy, ligamentum flavum change, canal/subarticular/foraminal stenosis and neural compromise at that level rather than repeating these later under separate structure headings.
- Keep normal level descriptions brief, e.g. **L1/2:** No significant disc bulge, spinal canal or foraminal stenosis.
- **Final checks:** After L5/S1, add only a concise survey of the paravertebral musculature and partially visualised sacroiliac joints, in that order. If an additional extraspinal finding was explicitly dictated, include it briefly after these checks; do not add a routine abdominal or retroperitoneal survey.
- Do not generate separate headings or repeated summaries for vertebral bodies, endplates, cauda equina, facet joints, ligamentum flavum, spinal canal or neural foramina. These belong in the overview or at the relevant level.
- Example order:
    - Lumbar alignment and marrow overview.
    - Conus termination and signal.
    - **L1/2:** ... through **L5/S1:** ...
    - Paravertebral musculature.
    - Partially visualised sacroiliac joints.
- **Commonly Misspelled Words:**
[correct spellings] spondylolysis, spondylolisthesis, foraminal stenosis, lateral recess, cauda equina, ligamentum flavum, Modic, annular fissure, protrusion, extrusion, sequestration, facet hypertrophy [correct spellings]
- If 'query' is mentioned in the transcript, it should be rendered as '?' in the report.

### Impression:
**Instructions:**
- Create an impression from findings, into a brief description in bullet points.
- Include only clinically relevant findings. Minor incidental findings that are fully described in the body do not need to be restated here.
- Translate moderate foraminal stenosis into potential irritation of the relevant exiting nerve root, and marked/severe foraminal stenosis into likely compression of that root, naming the side and root when the level permits. Do not make this inference for mild, canal or subarticular stenosis, or contradict an explicitly dictated nerve-root relationship.
- For a normal/negative study, include a closing statement where appropriate, e.g. 'No cause for the patient's symptoms identified on this examination.'
- **Sample:**
  - Lumbar spondylosis most significant at [level] with [disc protrusion / canal stenosis] affecting [nerve root].
  - No acute fracture or marrow signal abnormality.
