### THIS IS THE SAMPLE TEMPLATE TO BE USED FOR THE TRANSCRIPT ###

### Exam:
CT Lumbar Spine

### Technique:
Non-contrast CT of the lumbar spine.

### Clinical Details
**Instructions:**
- Record clinical details exactly as dictated. Do not rephrase or reformulate the wording.
- **Exclude** patient name, date of birth, age, gender, and MRN/accession numbers from the report, even if mentioned in the transcript.
- **If not provided:** 'No clinical details provided.'

### Comparison:
**Instructions:**
- **If provided:** List the prior imaging studies mentioned (modality and date/year if stated). Do not summarise changes here — comparison findings belong in the Findings section.
- **If not provided:** 'No prior imaging available for comparison.'

### Findings:
**Instructions:**
- Use this exact internal order within Findings: **overview first, then levels, then final checks**. Do not create extra structure-by-structure subsections.
- **Overview:** Begin with one concise unlabelled paragraph covering lumbar alignment and vertebral body heights, including any fracture, destructive osseous lesion or other non-level-specific bony finding. Preserve dictated wording when supplied; otherwise use compact normal wording.
- **Levels:** Then report L1/2, L2/3, L3/4, L4/5 and L5/S1 in anatomical order, regardless of dictation order. Give every level its own bold heading, e.g. **L4/5:**. Gather disc-space/osteophyte change, endplate and facet change, canal/subarticular/foraminal stenosis and neural compromise at the relevant level rather than repeating these later.
- Keep normal level descriptions brief, e.g. **L1/2:** No significant disc bulge, spinal canal or foraminal stenosis.
- **Final checks:** After L5/S1, add only a concise survey of the paravertebral musculature and sacroiliac joints, in that order. Include an extraspinal abdominal or retroperitoneal finding only when explicitly dictated; do not add a routine abdominal survey.
- Do not generate separate headings or repeated summaries for vertebral bodies, posterior elements, endplates, facet joints, spinal canal or neural foramina. These belong in the overview or at the relevant level.
- **Commonly Misspelled Words:**
[correct spellings] spondylosis, spondylolisthesis, spondylolysis, foraminal stenosis, lateral recess, cauda equina, annular fissure, facet hypertrophy, ligamentum flavum, disc desiccation, laminectomy [correct spellings]
- If 'query' is mentioned in the transcript, it should be rendered as '?' in the report.

### Impression:
**Instructions:**
- Create an impression from findings, into a brief description in bullet points.
- Include only clinically relevant findings. Minor incidental findings that are fully described in the body do not need to be restated here.
- Translate moderate foraminal stenosis into potential irritation of the relevant exiting nerve root, and marked/severe foraminal stenosis into likely compression of that root, naming the side and root when the level permits. Do not make this inference for mild, canal or subarticular stenosis, or contradict an explicitly dictated nerve-root relationship.
- For a normal/negative study, include a closing statement where appropriate, e.g. 'No cause for the patient's symptoms identified on this examination.'
- **Sample:**
  - Lumbar spondylosis with [level-specific] disc disease causing [mild/moderate/severe] canal/foraminal narrowing.
  - *No acute fracture identified.*
