### Exam:
CT Chest, Abdomen and Pelvis — Staging

### Technique:
Contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen and pelvis in the portal venous phase.

### Clinical Details
**Instructions:**
- Record clinical details exactly as dictated. Do not rephrase the wording.
- Exclude patient identifiers, age and gender from the report.
- If not provided: "No clinical details provided."

### Comparison:
**Instructions:**
- List the prior study type and date/year exactly as dictated.
- Put interval change in Findings, not in this section.
- If not provided: "No prior imaging available for comparison."

### Findings:
**Instructions:**
- Use only the two bold regional headings below and keep them in this exact order.
- Write a compact coherent paragraph under each heading. Do not add organ-by-organ subheadings.
- Preserve every dictated finding and measurement in the correct regional paragraph.
- For staging studies, give the site, size and interval change of the primary tumour, target lesions, suspicious nodes and metastases when dictated.
- Do not invent a cancer stage, treatment response, target lesion or interval change.
- Include precise normal coverage for the structures listed below when they were not dictated.

**Chest**
- Cover lungs and airways; pleura; mediastinal, hilar and axillary nodes; heart and pericardium; thoracic aorta and great vessels; chest wall; and bony thorax.
- Keep related normal structures together in one concise paragraph.

**Abdomen and Pelvis**
- Cover liver and biliary system; spleen; pancreas; adrenal glands; kidneys and ureters; bowel; mesentery and peritoneum; abdominal and pelvic nodes; vasculature; urinary bladder; pelvic organs; bones; and soft tissues.
- Keep related normal structures together in one concise paragraph.

### Impression:
**Instructions:**
- Give a concise staging summary in numbered points.
- Lead with the primary tumour and its interval change when known.
- Then summarise regional nodes, distant metastases and other clinically important findings.
- State treatment response only when supported by a suitable comparison and dictated findings.
- Do not include minor incidental findings unless clinically relevant.
