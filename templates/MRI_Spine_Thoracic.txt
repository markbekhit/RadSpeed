### THIS IS THE SAMPLE TEMPLATE TO BE USED FOR THE TRANSCRIPT ###

---

### Exam:
MRI Thoracic Spine

### Technique:
MRI of the thoracic spine was performed using T1-weighted, T2-weighted, and STIR sequences in sagittal and axial planes. [If contrast: Post-gadolinium T1-weighted sequences were also obtained.]

### Clinical Details
**Instructions:**
- Record clinical details exactly as dictated. Do not rephrase or reformulate the wording.
- **Exclude** patient name, date of birth, age, gender, and MRN/accession numbers from the report, even if mentioned in the transcript.
- **If not provided:** 'No clinical details provided.'

### Comparison:
**Instructions:**
- **If provided:** List the prior imaging studies mentioned (modality and date/year if stated). Do not summarise changes here — comparison findings belong in the Findings section.
- **If not provided:** 'No prior imaging available for comparison.'

### Findings:
**Instructions:**
- Use this exact internal order within Findings: **overview first, then levels, then final checks**. Do not create extra structure-by-structure subsections.
- **Overview:** Begin with one concise unlabelled paragraph covering thoracic alignment, kyphosis and vertebral body heights/bone marrow signal. State spinal cord calibre and signal, and conus position when included, in a concise sentence before the levels. Preserve dictated wording when supplied; otherwise use compact normal wording.
- **Levels:** Then report each abnormal thoracic disc level under its own bold heading in anatomical order from T1/2 to T12/L1, regardless of dictation order. Gather every comment for a level together, including disc/endplate/facet change, canal or foraminal stenosis and cord effect. Account for normal levels in one brief **Remaining levels:** line; do not itemise twelve normal levels separately.
- **Final checks:** Finish with one concise survey of the paravertebral soft tissues. Mention costovertebral joints, visualised lungs or upper abdominal structures only when explicitly dictated; do not add routine normal statements for them.
- Do not generate separate headings or repeated summaries for vertebral bodies, endplates, facet joints, ligamentum flavum, thecal sac, spinal canal or neural foramina. These belong in the overview or at the relevant level.
- **Commonly Misspelled Words:**
[correct spellings] Scheuermann disease, Schmorl node, syringomyelia, syrinx, transverse myelitis, demyelination, cord contusion, compression fracture, pars defect, costovertebral joint, paraspinal, epidural, extradural, intramedullary, extramedullary intradural, conus medullaris [correct spellings]
- If 'query' is mentioned in the transcript, it should be rendered as '?' in the report.

### Impression:
**Instructions:**
- Create an impression from findings, into a brief description in bullet points.
- Include only clinically relevant findings. Minor incidental findings that are fully described in the body do not need to be restated here.
- Translate moderate foraminal stenosis into potential irritation of the relevant exiting nerve root, and marked/severe foraminal stenosis into likely compression of that root, naming the side and root when the level permits. Do not make this inference for mild, canal or subarticular stenosis, or contradict an explicitly dictated nerve-root relationship.
- For a normal/negative study, include a closing statement where appropriate, e.g. 'No cause for the patient's symptoms identified on this examination.'
- **Sample:**
  - Mild thoracic spondylosis, most significant at [level].
  - No cord compression or intrinsic cord signal abnormality.
  - No compression fracture or marrow signal abnormality.
