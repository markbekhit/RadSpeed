### THIS IS THE SAMPLE TEMPLATE TO BE USED FOR THE TRANSCRIPT ###

### Exam:
MRI Cervical Spine

### Technique:
MRI of the cervical spine was performed using T1-weighted, T2-weighted, and STIR sequences in sagittal and axial planes. [If contrast: Post-gadolinium T1-weighted sequences were also obtained.]

### Clinical Details
**Instructions:**
- Record clinical details exactly as dictated. Do not rephrase or reformulate the wording.
- **Exclude** patient name, date of birth, age, gender, and MRN/accession numbers from the report, even if mentioned in the transcript.
- **If not provided:** 'No clinical details provided.'

### Comparison:
**Instructions:**
- **If provided:** List the prior imaging studies mentioned (modality and date/year if stated). Do not summarise changes here — comparison findings belong in the Findings section.
- **If not provided:** 'No prior imaging available for comparison.'

### Findings:
**Instructions:**
- Use this exact internal order within Findings: **overview first, then levels, then final checks**. Do not create extra structure-by-structure subsections.
- **Overview:** Begin with one concise unlabelled paragraph covering cervical alignment and vertebral body heights/bone marrow signal, followed by the craniocervical junction. State spinal cord calibre and signal in a concise sentence before the levels. Preserve dictated wording when supplied; otherwise use compact normal wording.
- **Levels:** Then report C2/3, C3/4, C4/5, C5/6, C6/7 and C7/T1 in anatomical order, regardless of dictation order. Give every level its own bold heading, e.g. **C5/6:**. Gather every comment for that level together. Include disc morphology and signal, endplate/uncovertebral/facet change, canal and foraminal stenosis, and cord or nerve-root effect at that level rather than repeating these later.
- Keep normal level descriptions brief, e.g. **C2/3:** No significant disc bulge, spinal canal or foraminal stenosis.
- **Final checks:** After C7/T1, add only a concise survey of the prevertebral soft tissues and paravertebral musculature, in that order. Include posterior fossa, upper thoracic or other extraspinal findings only when explicitly dictated; do not add them as routine normal headings.
- Do not generate separate headings or repeated summaries for vertebral bodies, endplates, uncovertebral joints, facet joints, spinal canal or neural foramina. These belong in the overview or at the relevant level.
- **Commonly Misspelled Words:**
[correct spellings] myelopathy, radiculopathy, spondylosis, spondylolisthesis, foraminal stenosis, disc osteophyte, cord signal, T2 hyperintensity, desiccation, uncovertebral, atlantoaxial, prevertebral [correct spellings]
- If 'query' is mentioned in the transcript, it should be rendered as '?' in the report.

### Impression:
**Instructions:**
- Create an impression from findings, into a brief description in bullet points.
- Include only clinically relevant findings. Minor incidental findings that are fully described in the body do not need to be restated here.
- For a normal/negative study, include a closing statement where appropriate, e.g. 'No cause for the patient's symptoms identified on this examination.'
- **Sample:**
  - Cervical spondylosis most marked at [level], with [mild/moderate/severe] central canal stenosis.
  - [Presence / Absence] of cord signal change to suggest myelopathy.
